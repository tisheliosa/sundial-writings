We came from the same fire. Two embers dancing in staccato playfulness on a balmy summer’s campfire. The fire pit glowed so warmly it could melt those gathered around into one. The fire twirled in the crystals of their eyes and they wished for it to be an everlasting show. We knew how each other moved so well that we didn’t for a second doubt, a gust would come through and vanish the flame. 

I have a friend I lovingly called Gags. To her, I was Gigs. We were Gigs and Gags, because that’s all we did around each other. There were a lot of things I’d imagined for a best friend 
- someone who I could endless yap to, heads rolling off the ends of hours spent wildly gesticulating on a sofa 
- Someone sharing laughter that split the sides of my stomach, mouth and brain from frivolous banter. They would clap and guffaw making me feel infinitely funnier than Jim Carrey in Dumb and Dumber 
- Someone who knew everything about you, the intensely dark bitter chocolate bits and the joyful sweet dough protecting it
- Someone w ho accepted my stingy reluctance to eat out and take me to 1915 Lanzhou noodle house for their free noodle portions 
- Someone whose brain I could tease the philosophies and wisdom from and admire 
- Someone who was willing to share when they’ve been hurt, why it did, and how I could be a more thoughtful friend, & vice versa. Where the difficult times made us stronger. 
- Someone who would share in a pair of nicknames with me 

I was never able to call someone a best friend so confidently until her. When I could, I felt like the luckiest person in the world. 

The gust felt like it came from nowhere. But it had been travelling across the unfurling seas and unsettling vineyard leaves to get here. When you lose a friend because of something you did, you feel like you lose your light. When the wind comes, the ashes scatter, the flames vanish, and the whole world becomes a grasping dark. 

I was throwing punches into the empty darkness, cold air meeting desperately closed fists. Keep moving, keep thinking, keep talking. It was the only way to ward off the loneliness and grief from completely swallowing me whole. Any moment I stayed still, the sadness gushed to fill my mind and eyes. The talisman was movement, occupation, busyness. But as I think and analyse more, replaying where I went wrong again and again, the stomach hollow expands, carving and turning out the flesh inside out. I felt sick, I wanted to vomit, my tears were inching and my chest felt tight. The busyness no longer worked. I  sit as a shell of a person, in front of a screen playing a strange reality of a Zoom meeting, and could do nothing but cry. From such dark and broken places, all sense and rationality abandons me and I can only lash out. A sour face, retreat, to only come out with words I don’t mean but yet will be seared into an imprint of regret that unchains any return to hopes of a resurrected friendship. Its death is final, the words you can’t retrieve as they fell patting down the last bit of the soil. 

My life is now laden with my mistreading, in the big shrink of my closest friends and their trust, losing trust, and feeling so at a loss. I know the only way on is to keep moving and keep finding. The forest has burned and ashened skies makes it hard to breathe, function normally. There is so much more life to live though, and for me to get there, I have to place a foot over the blackened fallen trunks and another, until I find where the field teems with bees and wildflowers again. Thank you for having been with me. I’ll miss you. I’m sorry. Goodbye.
