Melting into one world 

The crank to the screenplay of the first reunion back winds again and again. Each revision an imagination of what it would feel like for the mentally stitched pictures to bound alive from the reels. The script is reworked deliriously in anticipation for that first embrace. 

Seeing him was much better than in the pages of my loosely pencilled storyboard. No doubt, a stranger awkwardness like amateur actors meeting and performing their first scene together coloured our first hug when he picked me up from the train station. A hand not held in the car ride back. But we knew the act would progress where our sincere hearts would once more find and touch each others. 

He finds me in the kitchen, playing “more excitedly” with the dogs than having seen him. It’s not true, and a hug and a kiss where I pull away shyly holding him an arms distance away on both shoulders exposes my nervousness. It was like as if I was having my first kiss. 

When we laid down, there’s just a disbelief at the realness of this beautiful lover-turned-messages-turned-calls-turned-FaceTime-turned-stranger-turned-memories is actually warm, flesh, and pressure wrapping around me. Those arms became my world. Looking into those long lashed eyes, I saw them in proximity once more. He’s doing the same, and the searching eyes close the gaps of our longing hearts. There’s a disbelief in reuniting with him that is vastly distinguishable from seeing friends and family again. It was exciting to see the latter. But with him, he was my incessant daydreams, my object of longing, my one want. A broken tape record replaying to no other audience but one transfixed self. Now that he’s here, all of him, everything about him supersedes the mental shadows. Heat, smell, whispers in my ear. This is how I want to know him and feel him. I kiss him nonstop for all the times I couldn’t.
